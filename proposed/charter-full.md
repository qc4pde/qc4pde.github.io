
# QC4PDE Charter: Executive Summary

The Quantum Computing for Partial Differential Equations (QC4PDE) Consortium is
a pre-competitive, collaborative initiative established to determine whether
quantum computing can deliver practical value for partial differential
equations (PDE) and computational fluid dynamics (CFD).

## Why the Consortium exists

The charter begins from a clear-eyed assessment of the field: today there is no
proven case for quantum advantage in PDE/CFD, no dominant technical method, and
no established hardware path. The Consortium’s purpose is therefore not to
promote quantum computing, but to provide a rigorous, evidence-based framework
for evaluating where quantum, classical, or hybrid approaches may be
useful—and where they may not.

## Mission and approach

QC4PDE’s mission is to organize a shared research and benchmarking effort
focused on:

- identifying scientifically and industrially relevant PDE/CFD use cases,
- developing meaningful benchmark problems and workflows,
- fairly assessing quantum, classical, and hybrid methods,
- supporting realistic roadmap and stretch-goal analysis through sound
  resource estimation,
- and openly sharing methods, code, metadata, and results whenever possible.

The Consortium emphasizes a holistic systems perspective, evaluating full
workflows—not just isolated algorithms—including preprocessing, execution,
validation, postprocessing, orchestration, and hardware-software
considerations.

## Core principles

The Consortium is guided by several key principles:

- Scientific and industrial relevance should drive priorities.
- Outcome agnosticism is essential; negative findings are valuable.
- The effort is collaborative and non-competitive.
- It remains vendor-neutral and qubit-platform-neutral.
- It values both breadth and technical depth.
- It strongly prefers open science, open-source tools, and FAIR data
  practices.
- It accepts modern tools, including AI-assisted research and software
  engineering, when used transparently and rigorously.

## Scope and objectives

QC4PDE will study present-day and future-oriented methods across:

- canonical PDE/CFD benchmarks,
- increasingly ambitious stretch-goal problems,
- real scientific and industrial applications,
- end-to-end workflows,
- validation, performance, and resource-estimation methods.

Its objectives are to:

1. clarify credible pathways to utility,
2. define meaningful benchmarks,
3. compare methods systematically and fairly,
4. evaluate roadmaps and stretch goals rigorously,
5. improve validation practices,
6. preserve methods and results in reusable forms,
7. and build an active research community.

## Benchmarking and validation

A central feature of the Consortium is a staged benchmarking framework:

- starting with foundational textbook problems,
- progressing to stepped stretch goals,
- and eventually addressing broader real-world cases.

Benchmarks are expected to be well documented, including equations,
assumptions, discretizations, baselines, algorithm descriptions,
software/hardware environment, resource requirements, and performance metrics.

The charter also recognizes that validation in PDE/CFD cannot always rely
solely on direct comparison with classical reference solutions. It therefore
supports broader evaluation methods such as:

- analytical checks,
- convergence studies,
- conservation and physics-based checks,
- uncertainty quantification,
- cross-method comparisons,
- surrogate or reduced-order validation,
- and application-specific criteria.

## Openness, membership, and governance

The Consortium promotes FAIR data and metadata practices so that results remain
comparable over time, even as hardware and software evolve. It strongly favors
open dissemination, while acknowledging legal, contractual, export-control, IP,
and confidentiality limits.

Membership is open to participants from academia, industry, government labs,
research institutes, and technology providers who contribute to benchmarking,
evaluation, software, standards, use-case development, or community
activities.

Governance will be led by a Steering Committee, supported by Working Groups in
areas such as use cases, benchmarks, algorithms, validation, resource
estimation, software/metadata, and outreach.

## Expected outputs

Likely deliverables include:

- benchmark suites and specifications,
- open-source implementations and tools,
- comparative studies and technical reports,
- metadata standards,
- roadmap and resource assessments,
- workshops, white papers, publications, and shared repositories.

## Bottom line

The QC4PDE Consortium is designed to become a neutral, credible forum for
determining whether quantum computing can meaningfully impact PDE/CFD. Its
value lies in producing comparative evidence, practical guidance, shared
benchmarks, and a durable research community—including the important
possibility of showing where quantum utility is unlikely or not yet justified.
